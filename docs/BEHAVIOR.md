# Wisp — Behavior Reference

Detailed behavior and implementation notes. For an overview, see the [README](../README.md).

Original Rust Windows notch using Win32/GDI+, Windows media-session APIs, and the image crate. No forked application code.

## Run

`cargo build --release`, then open `target/release/wisp.exe`.

## Interface

Starts collapsed at 200×36 logical pixels showing only the clock; when playback starts (or a Pomodoro timer is active) the notch widens to 320×36 on the same spring that brings in the artwork and waveform, and narrows back when it stops. When music is playing, the collapsed view keeps the clock centered and adds the album artwork on the left and a compact playback activity indicator on the right; with lyrics on, the sung line takes the clock's place. When Spotify is paused or no music is playing, the collapsed view displays a sleek Apple-style clock (e.g. `14:32` in SF Pro Display Medium, centered horizontally and vertically in the notch) which updates live. Mode switching between Time Mode and Playing Mode in the collapsed state is governed by a dedicated critically damped spring (`play_progress`): when playback starts, the clock stays centered while the notch widens, the album artwork smoothly scales up and fades in, and the waveform indicator emerges; when playback pauses or stops, the artwork and waveform smoothly fade away and the notch narrows around the clock without visual pops. Moving the cursor over the notch automatically expands it into the 196-pt-tall panel (460 wide on Now Playing, 440 on Calendar, 640 on Shelf) without requiring a click; moving the cursor off the notch collapses it back. During expansion, the clock smoothly fades out as the expanded panel fades in; on collapse, the clock smoothly fades back in. Manual controls remain fully active: clicking controls (previous, play/pause, next) and timeline seek operate while expanded, clicking empty space on the top strip of a collapsed notch opens it (it never closes an expanded one, which would immediately reopen under the cursor), the scroll wheel or a horizontal touchpad swipe moves between pages, the notch has no right-click menu (right-clicking a shelf card reveals it in Explorer), and Escape collapses the panel.

Expansion and collapse share one critically damped spring (elapsed-time integration, approximately 0.55 seconds to settle). Cursor entry (WM_MOUSEMOVE with TrackMouseEvent) initiates automatic expansion, while cursor leave (WM_MOUSELEAVE) automatically collapses the panel; moving away or back mid-transition reverses the target without resetting position or velocity. The same artwork moves and scales between both layouts (22 px with 6 px corners collapsed, 72 px with 14 px corners expanded); expanded details fade and slide into place while the compact playback indicator fades along the moving right edge. Transport and timeline interactions are enabled only after expansion settles; refined translucent circular hover highlights appear on transport buttons with mathematically co-axial icon centering and active press feedback, and are cleared during transitions and on pointer exit. Wisp animates even when Windows “Animation effects” is off (many PCs turn it off for speed). With the tray option “Follow Windows animation setting” on, that setting is honored at startup and when it changes: with animations disabled the notch goes directly to its destination and the playback indicator remains still.

Expanded layout follows Apple's Now Playing: album artwork, title / artist / album, a thin white scrubber with elapsed and remaining time, and SF-Symbols-style backward / play-pause / forward glyphs. A small activity waveform tinted from the artwork's dominant color sits beside the title (and on the right of the collapsed notch). Spotify's thumbnail pads the cover with its logo underneath; only the cover block is kept. AC and battery information are removed. Media detection targets Spotify, Apple Music and Spotifast: Windows `GlobalSystemMediaTransportControlsSessionManager` sessions are filtered by `SourceAppUserModelId` containing "spotify", "applemusic" or "spotifast" (when several supported players are open: Windows' current media session while it plays, then the session already shown, then any playing one, then the current, shown or first paused one; the tray's “Music player” submenu can limit it to one app, and the transport buttons and scrubber always act on the session shown), ignoring browsers and other applications. If none exists, the idle state displays "Not playing" and "Play music on Spotify, Apple Music or Spotifast". Clicking the scrubber (it thickens on hover) requests a seek directly from the Spotify session; transport buttons directly issue skip previous, toggle play/pause, and skip next to that player via Windows Media Control APIs.

The Calendar page follows Apple's Calendar widget: red weekday label, a large date, month and year, and a Monday-first month grid with weekends dimmed and today in a red circle. Calendar dates are local system dates. The calendar shows dates only; there is no agenda or event list.

## To-do

The To-do page (checklist icon) is laid out like Apple Reminders. Click the "Add a task" field and type; Enter adds the task and keeps the field open for the next one, and Escape stops typing. While you type, the panel stays open even if the cursor leaves it; switching to another app or page ends typing. Each row has a circular checkbox: click a row to tick it (blue check, dimmed text) or click it again to untick. Hovering a row shows a delete button. "Clear Done" in the header removes ticked tasks. Four rows are visible; the wheel over the list scrolls longer lists. Tasks are saved to `%APPDATA%\Wisp\todos.txt` (one `0|1<TAB>text` line per task) after every change and loaded at startup.

## Lyrics & Volume

Now Playing's bottom row holds a lyrics button on the left (quote bubble), previous / play-pause / next in the middle, and the system volume on the right (speaker icon plus slider).

- **Lyrics** come from [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net). When the track changes, a worker thread (`src/lyrics.rs`) requests `/api/get` with artist, title, album and duration through the Windows `HttpClient`. If there is no exact match it moves on to looser searches: `/api/search` by title and artist, then the same with "(feat. …)" and "- Remastered"-style suffixes removed and only the first credited artist, then a free-text `q=` search. It stops at the first synced (or instrumental) result. If LRCLIB has no synced lyrics (or cannot be reached), Wisp falls back to NetEase Cloud Music's public web API: it searches for "artist title" and only accepts a result whose title (exactly, or without suffixes), artist and length (within 3 s) all match, then reads that song's LRC, dropping the credit lines at the top. Its pure-music flag counts as instrumental. NetEase errors and misses just keep LRCLIB's answer. Requests go out without cookies, because NetEase's search returns unrelated songs once its cookie is sent back. LRCLIB often answers 503 "server is busy"; that counts as a failed attempt, never as "no lyrics", and is retried with growing gaps (2, 5, 12, 25, 45 s, then every minute for about ten minutes). After the first busy answer the lyrics column says "LRCLIB is busy · Retrying…", and the lyrics appear on their own once LRCLIB answers. Results are cached in `%APPDATA%\Wisp\lyrics\` (one `.lrc` file per title and artist), so a song is only fetched once, even across restarts. "Not found" is cached for a day and failures are not cached. When the lyrics column says "No lyrics found" or "Couldn't reach LRCLIB", clicking the message asks LRCLIB again and skips the cache. What is sent is the track's title, artist, album and duration to LRCLIB, and the title and artist to NetEase only when LRCLIB has no synced lyrics.
- **Showing lyrics**: the button turns into a white disc; the panel springs taller (196 → 300), the artwork shrinks to a 44 pt thumbnail beside the title, and the lyrics column fades in. The sung line is bright and the others dim; as the song moves on, the column glides to the next line on a soft spring. Lines fade out at the top and bottom edges, instrumental gaps show three breathing dots, and clicking a line seeks to it. Messages cover loading, no match, unsynced-only, instrumental and network errors.
- **Collapsed lyrics**: while the lyrics button is on and a synced line is playing, the collapsed notch shows the sung line centered between the artwork and the waveform (13 pt Medium, with equal margins on both sides). When a line changes, the old one rises and fades as the next rises in, on the same spring as the expanded lyrics. The notch springs wider to fit long lines (up to 620 pt; longer lines end in "…") and narrows again for short ones. It switches back to the plain playing view during the intro, when paused, or when lyrics are off.
- **Timing**: players update the Windows timeline only now and then (Spotifast, for example, repeats the same position for long stretches), so the reported position is advanced by the time since its `LastUpdatedTime`. The UI runs its own clock, eases toward each sample that actually changed, and ignores repeated stale ones. It snaps on track changes, pauses and seeks, which also keeps the scrubber smooth. If a player only reports occasionally, lyrics can be off right after the notch starts until the next report arrives.
- **Volume** controls only the music player's own volume, its audio sessions in the Windows Volume Mixer, not the system level (`src/volume.rs`). Sessions are matched by process name for the current player (Spotify.exe, AppleMusic.exe, or a process whose name contains "spotifast"). Drag the slider, use the wheel over it, or click the speaker to mute. The speaker shows one to three waves by level and an × when muted. The control is hidden until the player has an audio session (it appears once playback starts); sessions are looked up again about every 2 s while Now Playing is open.

## Pomodoro

The Pomodoro page (timer icon) is laid out like the Apple Clock timer: a progress ring with the countdown and phase label, a Focus / Short / Long picker, session dots, an "Ends at" time, and Reset / Start-Pause-Resume pills (green to start, orange to pause). Focus runs 25 minutes, short breaks 5, and every fourth completed focus is followed by a 15-minute long break; these are defaults. Between sessions (not running and not paused part-way), an iOS-style − / + stepper right of the status line changes the selected phase's length: focus 5–90 minutes and long breaks 5–60 minutes in 5-minute steps, short breaks 1–30 minutes in 1-minute steps. A button greys out at the end of its range. The status line and the ring update at once, and the lengths are saved to `settings.txt` (`focus_minutes`, `short_minutes`, `long_minutes`) and restored at start-up. While a session runs or is paused, the stepper is hidden. While a timer is running or paused, the collapsed notch widens and shows a live countdown on the right (orange for focus, green for breaks), plus a small progress ring on the left when no music is playing. The timer tab also shows a small dot while it runs. When a phase ends, the notch chimes, opens on the Pomodoro page, and closes again after five seconds unless the cursor is over it. The timer is kept in memory and is not restored after a restart.

## File Shelf & Navigation

A native file holding shelf inspired by Apple Dynamic Island and macOS notch shelves:

- **Kept across restarts**: the shelf (files and folder groups, in order) is saved to `%APPDATA%\Wisp\shelf.txt` within about half a second of any change, and restored at startup. The shelf stores references to the files, not copies; files that no longer exist are dropped when it loads, and a group left with one file becomes a plain card.
- **Drag out to other apps**: drag a card out of the notch and drop it into Explorer, a browser, chat apps, editors and so on. Inside the notch a drag still merges cards into folders; once the cursor leaves the notch it becomes a standard Windows file drag (the same one Explorer uses), and the notch closes. Dragging a folder group carries all of its files, and inside a folder view a child starts the file drag right away. The drag offers copy and link only, so a drop into Explorer copies the file and the shelf keeps pointing at the original.

- **Native Drag & Drop & Drag-to-Expand**: Dragging files over the notch (even while collapsed) automatically expands the notch and activates the File Shelf via Windows OLE `IDropTarget` (`RegisterDragDrop`), previewing the expanded shelf drop zone before releasing. If the drag leaves the notch without dropping, the panel collapses back to the collapsed state smoothly; releasing mouse button adds the dropped files to the shelf and keeps the notch expanded on the shelf tab. Legacy Win32 `WM_DROPFILES` (`DragAcceptFiles` and `DragQueryFileW`) is also retained as fallback.
- **Header Navigation & Tabs**:
  - **Now Playing** (music note), **Calendar** (calendar icon), and **Shelf** (tray icon, with an item-count badge) are separate pages.
  - **No menus on the notch**: everything is reached from the header tabs, the drop zone (click to add files) and the header Clear button.
  - **Tray icon**: Wisp sits in the notification area as a small Dynamic Island glyph (white on a dark taskbar, black on a light one; it follows theme changes). Click or right-click it for its menu: **Settings…**, which opens the notch on its Settings page (it closes again after 8 seconds unless the cursor is on it), and **Quit Wisp**. On Windows 10 new tray icons start in the hidden overflow (the ^ arrow); drag the icon onto the taskbar to keep it visible. The icon is added back if Explorer restarts.
  - **Clear**: Header action button to quickly clear all stored items from the shelf.
- **Empty Drop Zone**: a quiet rounded drop zone with a tray icon, "Drop files here" and "or click to choose files"; clicking anywhere in it opens the native Windows file dialog (`GetOpenFileNameW`).
- **Interactive File Cards**:
  - Finder-style 120×118 tiles: no resting background, a subtle `#1E1E20` rounded highlight on hover.
  - macOS-like icons: a blue two-tone folder (groups show their item count), or a folded white page carrying a category glyph and the uppercase extension in the category color (Image, Audio, Code, Archive, Document, Generic).
  - Smart filename truncation preserving base name and file extension.
  - Formatted file size or "Folder" indicator.
  - Top-left gray circular close button ("✕"), macOS style, appearing on hover to remove individual items.
  - **Left Click**: Opens the file using native `ShellExecuteW`.
  - **Right Click**: Reveals the containing folder with the file selected in Windows File Explorer (`explorer.exe /select,path`).
  - **Horizontal Scroll**: When more than 4 items are stored, mouse wheel scrolling (`WM_MOUSEWHEEL`) smoothly navigates through cards.
- **Drag File into File & Folder Grouping**:
  - **Internal Drag-to-Merge**: On the Shelf view, users can drag any existing file card and drop it onto another file card to immediately merge them into a Folder.
  - **External Drop-to-Merge**: Dropping files directly onto an existing card (via native Windows OLE `IDropTarget` or `WM_DROPFILES`) automatically merges them with that card into a folder.
  - **Folder Card Presentation**: Displayed with a distinctive Folder badge icon, "Folder" title, and dynamic item count subtitle (`"2 items"`, `"3 items"`, etc.).
  - **Apple-Style Glowing Drag Feedback**: While dragging Card A over Card B (or hovering external files over a card), Card B displays a 2 px system-blue (`#0A84FF`) ring with a blue-tinted interior to indicate drop-to-merge, accompanied by an elevated floating card preview under the cursor.
  - **Folder View Navigation**: Clicking a Folder card opens the folder view inside the shelf. A top breadcrumb (`"‹  Shelf  /  Folder"`) allows returning to the top-level shelf by clicking or pressing `Escape`.
  - **Folder Child Interactions**: Inside the folder view, child cards retain individual category icons, file names, sizes, "✕" removal, left-click opening, and right-click reveal in Windows Explorer.
  - **Automatic Ungrouping**: If files in a folder are removed until only 1 remains, the folder automatically ungroups cleanly back into a standalone card on the shelf. If all files are removed, the folder is cleared.
- **Page Transitions**:
  - **Spring-driven hand-off**: a page switch runs one damped spring (`tab_t`, ω = 19, ζ = 0.86) from the old page to the new one. Pages slide 28 pt in the direction of travel; the outgoing page fades out over the first 35% while the incoming page fades in from 30%, so the two never overlap as a double exposure. Choosing a page mid-switch carries the motion over; going back reverses it with its momentum.
  - **Panel morph**: the panel width springs to each page's width (`panel_w`, ζ = 0.78 for a slight Dynamic Island-style settle). Pages are centered in the panel, so their content stays put on screen while the edges move.
  - **Header capsule**: the selected-tab capsule glides between tabs on the same spring, and the tab icons brighten with their page's opacity.
  - **Scroll wheel**: wheel down (or swipe right) moves to the next page, wheel up to the previous one, stopping at the ends. Each gesture moves one page; touchpad deltas accumulate to one notch and input during a switch is ignored. On a shelf with more than four items, the wheel over the cards scrolls them instead.
  - Interactions are enabled once the page and width springs settle; with Windows animations disabled, switches are instant.
  - **Item Badge**: a neutral gray count pill next to the shelf tab icon.
Appearance: pure black with Apple dark-mode label colors — `#F5F5F7` primary, `#98989E` secondary, `#636368` tertiary — a white scrubber on a `#343437` track, system red `#FF453A` for the calendar and system blue `#0A84FF` for drop targets. Uses installed SF Pro Display: 15px Medium / 14px Regular / 12px Regular / 11px Medium / 40px Regular (calendar date). The installed Semibold face is italic, so Medium is intentionally selected for upright headings. Font files are not redistributed. Install SF Pro Display separately before using this build on another machine. If it is missing, Wisp detects the substitution at startup and uses Inter instead: Regular and Medium are embedded in wisp.exe (SIL Open Font License) and loaded privately for the process, so nothing is installed. Segoe UI is used only if that fails.

Header and drop-zone icons are SF Symbols-style line glyphs (music.note, calendar, list.bullet, stopwatch, tray.and.arrow.down) drawn as antialiased 1.6 pt GDI+ strokes with round caps and joins; the renderer requests 8×8 antialiasing and falls back to the standard mode. All interior shapes use native GDI+ antialiased paths: progress track/fill, calendar selection, transport controls, activity bars, and header/calendar/music icons. Capsules use half their smaller dimension as the radius; the 24px selected date is a true circle. Artwork uses a cached image texture painted through the antialiased rounded path, with high-quality resampling rather than a hard GDI clipping region. SF Pro Display text retains grayscale font antialiasing (not colored subpixel fringes). The silhouette keeps 6px concave top shoulders, and its lower radii open from 10px collapsed to 26px expanded as the panel grows, with fractional coverage integrated vertically along the edge before premultiplied-alpha presentation through UpdateLayeredWindow.

Rendering reuses the full-size BGRA DIB, GDI+ graphics context, path, solid brush, artwork texture, and UTF-16 conversion buffer. Artwork resources change only when the media artwork changes; no blanket high-resolution supersampling buffer is used. GDI+ is loaded from the Windows system DLL through cached exports, so the GNU Rust build does not require a separate GDI+ import library. Text and native drawing are synchronized before the layered-window pixels are presented.

Frames are driven by DwmFlush on a worker, with one synchronous UI message per compositor cycle (no queued backlog or fixed 16ms timer). Only motion of the notch itself (opening, closing, resizing, page switches) is locked to DwmFlush. If DwmFlush stops pacing (it can return immediately while the display is off or the session is locked), the worker falls back to a timer, so the UI thread is never flooded and repaints are never starved. While only the waveform or a lyric line moves, or the panel sits open, frames come from a high-resolution timer at the display refresh rate instead, since DwmFlush costs noticeable CPU on some drivers. While nothing moves (collapsed, no music playing, no notification or drag), it slows to about 10 ticks a second, enough for the clock, notifications and media updates; any mouse or keyboard input brings it back to full rate within 10 ms. Each frame only does coverage math on the silhouette band. Text, icons and the artwork are cached once rendered: text as grayscale masks per string, font and box, icons per glyph and quarter-pixel position, and the rounded artwork at its current size. A frame of the open Now Playing panel with lyrics takes about 0.65 ms instead of about 2.9 ms; GDI text with SF Pro alone cost ~100 µs a string. The media worker keeps one Windows media-session manager for its lifetime; requesting a new one every second leaked a little memory each time. This follows Windows compositor cadence, which may differ from physical refresh on mixed-refresh displays or remote sessions. The spring uses an exact elapsed-time update rather than fixed frame steps, with a small position/velocity settling threshold. Content fading is composited against the black interior, reusing the existing drawing resources without additional surfaces.

## Settings page

The sixth page (gear tab alone at the right end of the header, with Clear / Clear Done just left of it; also reachable with the scroll wheel) is 440×300 and follows iOS/macOS dark-mode settings.

- **Cards:** two inset grouped cards (#1C1C1E, 12 pt corners) under 11 pt section labels in the secondary label color, so they stay above 4.5:1 contrast. "Notch" holds Open on hover, Shrink over content, Lyrics when closed and Notifications; "General" holds 24-hour clock, Match Windows motion and Start with Windows, with a "Changes save automatically." note below.
- **Rows:** 32 pt tall, separated by hairlines. The whole row is the click target. Hover and press tint an inset rounded highlight without moving anything.
- **Switches:** iOS-style, 34×20, off #39393D, on system green. The knob slides on a spring (ω 24, ζ 0.82).
- **Music player:** a full-width segmented control (Automatic, Spotify, Spotifast, Apple Music) whose thumb glides to the choice (ω 20, ζ 0.86).
- **Footer:** the version, and a Quit Wisp button.

Everything is saved to `%APPDATA%\Wisp\settings.txt` (`key=0|1`, `player=…`) as soon as it changes. Start with Windows writes or removes a `Wisp` value under `HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run`, and its switch reflects that value. With reduced motion, the switches and segment jump instead of sliding.

## Minimal mode

When the closed notch would hide something, it shrinks to a 120×6 sliver at the top edge, so things like browser tabs that reach it stay visible and clickable. An empty title bar or tab strip behind the notch doesn't count. Four times a second, `update_cover` walks the window z-order from the top. It skips Wisp itself, hidden, minimized and DWM-cloaked windows, and click-through overlays (`WS_EX_TRANSPARENT`, such as the NVIDIA overlay). It then checks the first window that overlaps the notch's normal closed footprint (its full width × 36 pt, never the sliver, so the check can't flip itself back and forth). If that window is the desktop (`Progman`/`WorkerW`), the notch isn't covered. If it is an app window, Wisp then looks at the screen pixels: two 14 pt strips just left and right of the notch while it is normal, plus the area around and under the sliver once it has shrunk. Each area is judged on its own, and it counts as empty if at least 97% of its pixels are within a small distance of its most common color (`is_flat`, unit tested). Judging them separately matters: pooled, a few letters in a strip would vanish into the large area under the sliver, and the notch would keep shrinking and growing back. Text, icons, tab edges, or two differently colored sides count as content. So the notch shrinks when tabs reach its edges, and grows back only when the whole area it would cover is clear again. Shrinking needs content seen twice in a row (about half a second). Growing back needs a clear area eight checks in a row (about two seconds). After any switch, the opposite one waits at least three seconds. While shrunk, the checked width is the widest the notch has been since it shrank, so a shorter lyric line can't make it pop back out.

`mini_t` springs the collapsed size to `MINI_W`×`MINI_H` (ω 19, ζ 0.86) while the clock, artwork, waveform, lyric line and timer fade out. Hovering or clicking the sliver opens the full panel as usual, and notifications still spring open from it. The bottom corner radius is limited to half the window height so the sliver keeps the notch's shape. "Shrink when covering a window" in the tray turns it off.

## Notifications

`src/notify.rs` reads new toasts from the Action Center's database, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\Windows\Notifications\wpndatabase.db`. It opens the file read-only through the system's `winsqlite3.dll`, which is loaded at runtime, so nothing extra is linked or installed. A worker thread polls every 0.7 s for rows in `Notification` with `Type = 'toast'` and a higher `[Order]` than the last one seen. Toasts that were already there at start-up, or that arrived more than 30 s before they were seen, are skipped.

From the toast XML, the first `<text>` becomes the title and the rest the body; attribution text is dropped. An `appLogoOverride` image is used when it is a local file (Discord sends the sender's avatar this way), drawn as a circle when the toast asks for `hint-crop="circle"` and as a rounded square otherwise. Otherwise the app's name and icon come from the shell's Apps folder (`shell:AppsFolder\<AUMID>`), the same ones Start shows. They are cached per app.

When a notification arrives and the notch is collapsed, `notice_t` springs the collapsed notch from its strip to a 380×68 pill with a slight Dynamic Island overshoot (ω 17, ζ 0.72), while the clock, artwork, waveform, lyrics and timer fade out. The pill shows a 38 pt icon, the title (15 pt Medium), one line of the message (14 pt, secondary) and the app's name (11 pt Medium, tertiary). Long text ends in "…".

It stays for 5 s, or 3 s when more are waiting; at most four are kept in the queue. Hovering holds it, and leaving gives it another 1.2 s. Clicking opens the app through `shell:AppsFolder` and dismisses the pill. While a notification shows, hovering does not expand the panel; notifications that arrive while the panel is open wait until it closes.

The database is undocumented, so every step fails quietly: if it is missing or its layout changes, the notch simply shows no notifications. Windows keeps showing its own banners; turning those off in Settings leaves notifications in the Action Center, where Wisp still reads them. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

## Verification

`cargo run --release -- --smoke-test` checks native expansion/collapse dimensions (460×196 Now Playing / 200×36 clock-only), top-edge placement, shelf integration (adding, switching tabs, clearing), and collapsed mode transition (clock <-> playing modes) after the springs settle, then exits and writes `smoke-result.txt`; a four-second timeout reports failure instead of hanging.



Windows 10/11, primary display only. No forked app source; Wisp only starts with Windows if "Start with Windows" is turned on in Settings.
